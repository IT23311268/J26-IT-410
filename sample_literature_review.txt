Recent advances in transformer-based architectures have substantially improved the performance of natural language processing systems across a wide range of tasks (Vaswani et al., 2017). In particular, BERT and its variants have demonstrated state-of-the-art results on reading comprehension and text classification benchmarks. However, several studies have reported that large language models frequently generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect statements, a phenomenon commonly referred to as hallucination [1]. One study found that GPT-3 achieved an accuracy of 87% on the TruthfulQA benchmark, significantly outperforming earlier models. Furthermore, retrieval-augmented generation methods have been shown to reduce hallucination rates by approximately 40% compared to standard generation approaches (Lewis et al., 2020). The computational cost of training these models remains prohibitively high for many research institutions, with some estimates suggesting training costs exceeding one million dollars per run.
